\title{A Statistical Theory of Daily-Bar Volatility under a Noisy Open\\[4pt]
\large The theory section and Appendix B of\\ \emph{When the Open Overreacts: Measuring Daily Volatility in Frontier Markets without Options}}
\author{Anam Giri}
\date{Working paper, October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The daily bar's opening price can carry a transient error that the session undoes, as it does in
Nepal's auction-set open. This document develops the statistics of that problem and states six
propositions, with complete proofs in Appendix~B. It shows what the unbiasedness coefficient
$b=\E[or]/\E[o^2]$ identifies, with a sharp lower bound on the error that replaces a weaker
published one. It derives exactly how much of the error each classical range estimator absorbs,
and how a price band inflates $b$, with a closed-form factor. It explains why one coefficient
pooled across securities whose opens differ penalises the estimator that reads the open. Finally,
it shows how a rolling calibration lags a rule change, and when lagged realised measures identify
an estimator's calibration. Each closed form, identity and inequality was checked against
simulation or numerical integration by \texttt{scripts/45\_theory\_checks.py}. The ledger has
\thn{checks-n} checks and \thn{checks-pass} pass. The applications to the NEPSE data and the one
new test were fixed in advance in plan M19.
\end{abstract}
