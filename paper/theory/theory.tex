\title{A Statistical Theory of Daily-Bar Volatility under a Noisy Open\\[4pt]
\large The theory section and Appendix B of\\ \emph{When the Open Overreacts: Measuring Daily Volatility in Frontier Markets without Options}}
\author{Anam Giri}
\date{Working paper, October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The daily bar's opening price can carry a transient error that the session undoes, as it does in
Nepal's auction-set open. This document develops the statistics of that problem and states eight
propositions, with complete proofs in Appendix~B. It shows what the unbiasedness coefficient
$b=\E[or]/\E[o^2]$ identifies, with a sharp lower bound on the error that replaces a weaker
published one. It derives exactly how much of the error each classical range estimator absorbs,
and how a price band inflates $b$, with a closed-form factor. It explains why one coefficient
pooled across securities whose opens differ penalises the estimator that reads the open. Finally,
it shows how a rolling calibration lags a rule change, and when lagged realised measures identify
an estimator's calibration. A seventh proposition, added after an independent audit, shows what Anam's
estimator does with a noisy open. Its overnight term understates the efficient overnight variance
unless the error is proportional to the news. Its extended range keeps any error that set the high or
the low. Its open-free form still absorbs part of the error's variance: between \thn{k7-xOF-min} and
\thn{k7-xOF-max} of it at the error sizes the data imply, against about half for Parkinson. Each closed form, identity and inequality was checked against
simulation or numerical integration by \texttt{scripts/45\_theory\_checks.py}. That ledger has
\thn{checks-n} checks, and \thn{checks-pass} pass. Every step of every proof was then verified by
\texttt{scripts/46\_theory\_proofs.py}, symbolically with SymPy wherever the step is algebra or
calculus. Of its \thn{proofs-n} steps, \thn{proofs-pass} pass. The seventh proposition's closed forms
were checked by \texttt{scripts/48\_kernel\_theory.py} (\thn{k7-checks-n} checks, \thn{k7-checks-pass}
passing). An eighth proposition explains why a single coefficient fails. In a panel, the open's reliability
is a weighted mean of two parts: a market part, most of which the session keeps, and a stock-specific part of
which it keeps less. The market's move can be read from the other securities' opens, free of the security's
own error. Its checks are in \texttt{scripts/54\_market\_open\_theory.py} (\thn{m8-checks-n} checks,
\thn{m8-checks-pass} passing). The applications to the NEPSE data
and the one new test were fixed in advance in plan M19.
\end{abstract}
